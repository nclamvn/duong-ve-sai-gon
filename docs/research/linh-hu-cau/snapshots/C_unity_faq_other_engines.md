> **Short answer:** Yes. The article says you can use Unity Asset Store assets with other engines "as long as you comply with the Unity Asset Store End User License Agreement (EULA)." Section 2 of the EULA covers the rules for purchased assets.
> 
> **Restrictions (paraphrased):**
> - **Redistribution:** You can't sell or share assets as standalone resources, and users of your project shouldn't be able to extract the raw asset files.
> - **Cost sharing:** You can't split a purchase with others and let them use the asset.
> - **User-generated content:** If your project lets users create content (such as a level editor), you can't monetize the asset without the creator's explicit permission.
> - **Primary purpose:** Your project can't be mainly about letting others access or generate content with the asset.
> 
> **Additional conditions:**
> - **Restricted assets** may have separate terms, such as "Restricted Asset Terms," so check the included documentation.
> - **Open-source components** may fall under their own licenses. If the EULA conflicts with an open-source license, the open-source license prevails.
> 
> The article recommends reviewing each asset's licensing terms before use.
