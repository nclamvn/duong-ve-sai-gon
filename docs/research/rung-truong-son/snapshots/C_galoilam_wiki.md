I can't copy the full passages verbatim because of the 125-character limit on quotes from source documents. Below are shorter exact quotes, with paraphrase where needed. The page has no elevation information.

**Size:** "The bird's length is 58–65 centimetres (23–26 in)". The page also gives male tail length as about 24–26 cm and female as about 20–22 cm, and says males are heavier.

**Plumage:** "The male is mainly blue-black with a crest, and the female is a drab brown bird." Females are described as chestnut-brown and without a crest.

**Distribution:** "Edwards's pheasant is endemic to central Vietnam", with Thua Thien Hue listed among four provinces (Ha Tinh, Quang Binh, Quang Tri, and Thua Thien Hue). Quang Binh and Quang Tri were also the focus of a 2010 forest survey.

**Habitat:** "They live in evergreen forests with lots of palms and patches of bamboo." The page adds that females nest on the forest floor.

**Behaviour:** "The birds often hide in the low vegetation." The page notes that the species is rarely observed in the wild, so its natural behaviour is poorly known. It also says the birds do not commonly migrate.

**Call:** "Their alarm call is a low guttural "uk uk uk uk uk"", sometimes a harder "puk puk puk puk puk".

**Status:** "Critically endangered, possibly extinct in the wild (IUCN 3.1)". The page also says: "It is considered possibly extinct in the wild as a result."

**History of records:** "Edwards's pheasant was first observed in 1896." The page says: "They have not been spotted in the wild since the year 2000." It also reports that in 2018 "a photograph of a dead female Edwards's pheasant was taken in A Lưới District", the first evidence of a wild bird in almost two decades.
